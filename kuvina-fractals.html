Totalistic cellular automaton: each cell computed from its left, center, and right neighbors modulo N. State 0 = black, others = hue wheel.

A direct implementation of the algorithm from Kuvina Saydaki's How I made my own Fractal. The favicon and header logo are the left half of the N=4 a+b+c automaton, cropped at the centre line to a 16×16 icon.

Kuvina Fractals implements the algorithm from Kuvina Saydaki's video How I made my own Fractal (@Kuvina). The rule families, hue-wheel palette and mod-N arithmetic are as they describe them; the custom-rule language, presets and exporters are this page's additions. The favicon and header logo are the left half of the N=4 a+b+c automaton, cropped at the centre line to a 16×16 icon.
Primes vs Composites
When N is prime, every non-zero state is reachable and the automaton produces unique, non-repeating patterns. The ring ℤp has no zero divisors, so the totalistic rule explores the full state space.

When N is composite, the pattern reveals the factor structure. For example N=6 = 2×3 produces interference between the N=2 and N=3 sub-patterns, and N=4 = 2² shows nested copies of the Rule 150 triangle.
