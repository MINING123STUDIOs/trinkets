=======================================================================
  LANDSCAPE EXPLORER - A BEGINNER'S GUIDE
=======================================================================

Hello, and welcome. This little document explains everything about the
game in plain words. No jargon, no programming talk. Just what things
are and how to have fun with them.


=======================================================================
1. WHAT IS THIS?
=======================================================================

Landscape Explorer is an infinite world you can wander through. The
computer builds every part of it on the spot, out of a "recipe" - so
the world never ends and no two worlds are quite the same.

There is no install, no account, no internet needed. The whole game is
one single file called landscape-explorer.html. Just open it in a web
browser (double-click it, or drag it into a browser window) and you are
in. It works on Windows, Mac and Linux.

A few simple ideas you will meet again and again:

  WORLD - the ground and walls you walk on, drawn as a grid of small
          squares.

  MODE  - one of 53 different "kinds of world". Some modes are mazes,
          some are caves, some are neat tile patterns, and so on. You
          can switch modes at any time.

  SEED  - the secret number the computer uses to build the world.
          Change the seed and you get a brand-new world with the same
          mode. It's like shuffling the deck.

  CELL  - one tiny square of the world. You step from cell to cell.


=======================================================================
2. HOW BIG, AND HOW FAST
=======================================================================

Everything in the world is built on one simple rule: ONE CELL IS ONE
METRE. That is not just flavour text - it means every number the game
shows you stands for a real distance you can feel.

  - Walk speed starts at 1 cell per second = 1 metre per second, which
    is a calm walking pace (about 3.6 km/h, 2.2 mph). You can dial it
    up or down with /speed; it has no upper limit.
  - At that pace you cross a football pitch (100 m) in about 100
    seconds, and one built-up block of terrain - a "chunk", 32 m wide -
    in about 30 seconds.
  - The HUD's velocity meter reports the same figure three ways when
    help is up: metres per frame (cpf), metres per second (cps) and
    kilometres per hour (kcph). Target and measured are both in this
    scale.

HOW FAR CAN YOU GO?
The world is drawn on the fly, never stored, so there is no fixed map
edge. The only limit is how big a coordinate the browser can count to.
On Firefox that ceiling is a number with about 315,000 digits; on
Chrome/Edge/Node it towers astronomically higher, a number with about
323 million digits. Either way it is far, far beyond the size of the
observable universe (just ~10^26 metres). Ordinary positions - most of
what you will ever visit - live well below 10^11 cells, which at one
metre per cell is a region wider than the whole Earth-to-Sun orbit
(about 1.3 AU).

ICE MODE, THE PHYSICS LESSON
Press J to freeze the floor. Sliding accelerates you forever - there is
no top speed. At default speed that is a real ~20 m/s^2 of constant
push: about ten minutes on ice reaches roughly 12 km/s (over 43,000
km/h) and eats up ~3,600 km of corridor - enough to cross a continent.
If you had a quarter light-year of straight ice you would get all the
way up to the speed of light (it would take you about 174 days of
constant sliding). Gameplay rarely lets you build that much room, but
the numbers are honest.


=======================================================================
3. STARTING OUT
=======================================================================

When the game opens you are standing in a random landscape, looking at
it from above. If you don't move anything, you will see a few hints on
screen.

The single most useful key is:

    Shift+H    opens the full help screen (press it again or Escape to
               close)

Press that any time you forget what a key does.


=======================================================================
4. TWO WAYS TO PLAY
=======================================================================

You can play the game in two completely different moods:

  A) THE EXPLORER (free camera)
     The default. The camera is like a floating map - you can zoom in
     and out and drag it anywhere. Nothing blocks you. Great for just
     looking at the beautiful patterns.

  B) THE ADVENTURER (on foot)
     Press U and you now walk the world as a little square person.
     Walls stop you - you can only walk along open ground. This is
     where the excitement lives: goals to reach and hunters to avoid.

Press U any time to switch between the two. Both are equally valid;
the game never forces either on you.


=======================================================================
5. MOVING AROUND
=======================================================================

All the keys you need for getting around:

  WASD  or  ARROW KEYS     Move (in adventurer mode: walk. In explorer
                           mode: slide the camera around)

  I  and  K                Zoom in / zoom out

  MOUSE WHEEL              Zoom, centred on where the cursor is

  DRAG  (hold mouse)       Pan the camera in explorer mode

  P  and  N                Move faster / slower (both for walking and
                           camera)

  TAB                      Teleport. In adventurer mode you blink to a
                           far-away random spot (good for escaping a
                           tight corner)

  Shift+Tab                Teleport back to the very start of the
                           world

   Ctrl+Shift+Spacebar      Teleport to the nearest big open space
                           (useful if you are stuck inside a wall)

   Shift+S                 Set your spawn point to where you are
                           standing now (you respawn here after
                           getting caught)

   R                        Shuffle the deck - build a brand-new world
                           from the same mode, right where you are

   Space                    Advance hunters one step (hold it to keep
                           them moving). On mobile: the Hunt button.

If you get hopelessly lost, press Tab. There is always a way out.


=======================================================================
6. WORLD TYPES - THE 53 MODES
=======================================================================

A "mode" is the recipe for a kind of landscape. There are 53 of them,
sorted into families. Each family is a "bin": hold an F-key (or Shift /
Ctrl) and press a digit 1 to 9 to switch worlds instantly. Digit 0 and
digits past a family's size do nothing; F12 is reserved (it opens the
browser's developer tools).

  FAMILY 1: MAZES                      (F1 / F2 + digits)
     F1+1 maze, F1+2 braid, F1+3 recursive; F2+1 hilbert, F2+2
     hilbertinv, F2+3 megamaze, F2+4 megamazep60. Perfect mazes where
     every path leads somewhere, twisty tunnels. Think: puzzles.

  FAMILY 2: MINES & TILES              (F3 / F4 + digits)
     F3+1 mine, F3+2 blobs, F3+3 mineshaft; F4+1 tenprint, F4+2 truchet,
     F4+3 qr, F4+4 glyphs. Mine networks, then neat repeating patterns:
     carpet-like tiles, arrow glyphs, and even giant QR codes.

  FAMILY 3: SNOW & PERCOLATION         (F5 / F6 + digits)
     F5+1 snowyplanes (a vast snowy plain); F6+1 critical, F6+2 critbond
     (worlds at the edge of collapse).

  FAMILY 4: CAVES                      (F7 / F8 + digits)
     F7+1 cavern, F7+2 plasma, F7+3 dripstonecave; F8+1 cheese, F8+2
     spaghetti, F8+3 noodle, F8+4 pillar, F8+5 warp, F8+6 carver,
     F8+7 ravine. Soft organic caves: big caverns, cheese pockets,
     spaghetti noodles, tall pillar halls, deep canyons.

  FAMILY 5: MOUNTAINS, CELLS & FORESTS (F9 / F10 / F11 + digits)
     F9+1 mountains; F10+1 voronoi, F10+2 cellular, F10+3 cellnoise,
     F10+4 conway, F10+5 crypt, F10+6 conwaygallery, F10+7 babel;
     F11+1 weierstrass (a never-smooth fractal). Ridged peaks and
     structured cell worlds; F11+6 chip (silicon-wafer interference bands).

  ROOMS, WORLDS & TREES               (Shift / Ctrl + digits)
     Shift+1 chambers, Shift+2 uchambers, Shift+3 colapsedchambers
     (rooms with doorways);
     Shift+Ctrl+1 underground (blend several cave styles); Ctrl+1 forest, Ctrl+2 forestcave (bigger dramatic worlds);
     Ctrl+3 metropolis, Ctrl+4 village, Ctrl+5 subdivisions (settlements,
     from a tight city of streets to loose country lanes and estates);
     plain 1 = random (walk anywhere).

Other ways to change the world:

  O                      Cycle through all 53 modes, one at a time
                         (hold Shift+O to go backwards, Ctrl+O jumps
                         to a random mode)

C                      Change the color theme (there are eight:
                           default, dark, ocean, ice, darkice, solardark,
                           solarlight and inverted).
                           C+digit jumps to a theme by number.
                           In ice mode, C switches between ice and darkice.

  R                      New seed = a fresh world of the same mode

A quick tip: there is no wrong way to browse. Flick through the modes
with O and stop when one makes you say "whoa".


=======================================================================
7. GOALS AND CHASING  (the star game)
=======================================================================

If you want a little purpose, press:

  X                      Place a goal

A big star is placed far away from you - roughly 500 steps - and a
little "goal" counter appears. Now walk (or fly the camera) to the
star. Reaching it:

  - scores you a point (the counter goes up)
  - places the next star even farther away
  - and so on, forever. Each goal is about 1.5x farther than the last,
    until it hits a generous maximum.

It is a simple "go see what's out there" game. In explorer mode you can
just glide there. In adventurer mode you have to actually find the
path - which is where the hunters come in (next section).


=======================================================================
8. THE HUNTERS  (the red triangles)
=======================================================================

When you are in adventurer mode, the world is not empty. Red triangles
start to chase you. They are drawn like you and each one points its
nose straight at you.

Their rules are simple and fair:

  - They move exactly when you move: one step for every step you take
    (a diagonal move counts as two steps). Stop, and they freeze.
  - That means a steady walk never lets them get closer - but every
    time you stop or turn around, they gain ground.
  - They always know roughly where you are, but they can only *see*
    you in a straight line. Step around a corner and they lose you.
  - Occasionally (about 7.5% of the time) a hunter stumbles and misses
    its step, giving you a little breathing room.

How to survive:

  - Keep moving. Standing still is how you get caught.
  - Break line of sight: duck around a corner or a wall, change
    direction, double back.
  - Tab-teleport is your panic button.

Getting caught costs a life. You have 3 lives. Lose all 3 and you
respawn fresh with 3 lives again - nothing is ever permanently lost.

If you would rather be alone:

  E                      Turns hunters on and off (off by default).

  /enemies 2             (in the terminal) sets how many hunt you,
                         from 1 up to 8. See section 8.


=======================================================================
9. LANTERN MODE  (the flashlight)
=======================================================================

Press L and night falls around you. You hold a flickering warm lantern
that lights a pool of ground around you. Walls cast real shadows into
the darkness.

In lantern mode, hunters are hidden in the dark. You can only see one
when it walks into your light. They may be anywhere just outside the
pool of light, waiting.

  Shift+Y   Toggles "hunter teleport" (off by default). With it on,
            hunters that drift far from your light are quietly pulled
            back to the very edge of the pool - so they circle you
            just outside what you can see. Turn this off and they
            stay exactly where they are, free to lurk in the dark and
            creep up on foot.

Lantern mode is the spookiest way to play. Turn the sound up, turn
Shift+Y off, and see how long you last.


=======================================================================
10. ICE MODE  (the slide)
=======================================================================

Press J and the world turns to ice. A fresh press of any direction
key starts a slide:

  - You accelerate at a fixed rate forever - there is no top speed
    cap, so a long straight corridor lets you really wind up.
  - You keep going in that one direction until you hit a wall -
    mid-slide keypresses are ignored, so commit to your path.
  - While sliding you dash straight through any hunter in your path.
    But hunters anticipate you: they race toward the wall where your
    slide is going to end, so don't blindly commit to long corridors.
  - Pressing J again turns it off and restores the normal theme.
    If you were in a dark theme (dark or darkice) you stay in the
    dark family: J turns the world dark-ice instead of light ice.

In ice mode you can cycle the theme with C: light ice or darkice, a
deep frozen-night variant. The two ice themes are only reachable
while ice mode is on.

Ice mode is fast and a little reckless. The hunters still chase you
at their own pace - they cannot match your slide, but they anticipate
where it will end and head for that wall. Short slides and quick
turns keep them guessing; a long, straight corridor can leave the
pack waiting right where you stop.

  /ice                 (in the terminal) toggles ice mode too.


=======================================================================
11. VISION MODE  (the radar)
=======================================================================

Press V and the world goes into "radar" mode: the screen fills with the
current theme's floor color (so the default theme turns it black, the new
inverted theme white) and a ring of single pixels - in the theme's wall
color, one exactly where each ray first strikes - marks precisely where the
walls around you are, one dot for every direction the game can "see" out to
`R` cells. The goal and each hunter absorb rays on exactly the same shapes
they have in normal mode - the goal's small diamond, an enemy's triangle -
stamping the ray's entry point onto that shape in the same wall-colored dots,
so an enemy appears as a speckled silhouette of its normal-mode outline (no
marker drawn on top). Rays that thread past the shape pass through the cell,
so a goal or enemy hides only what lies directly behind it - a true
line-of-sight picture instead of the full bright world of normal
play.

  The dots never fade with distance: every one is a solid wall-colored
  pixel, so the radar reads as a sharp outline of what is around you.
  Rays also stop at the screen edge - anything a ray would hit beyond
  the visible screen is not drawn, which keeps long ranges cheap.

  - Pressing V turns the radar on (it also puts you into player mode
    and spawns you at the center of the screen, like ice and the
    lantern do). Press V again to switch back to normal rendering.
  -   /vision         (in the terminal) toggles it too.
  - /vision 128       Sets how far the rays reach, in cells (8-256,
                      default 64). Bigger range = more of the map, but
                      also more rays and more dots.
  - If you hit U (free-fly) while the radar is on, rays come from the
    mouse cursor instead of your player - handy for poking around the
    map before committing.
  - The lantern and the radar are rivals: turning one on switches the
    other off (blowing out the lantern also restores the theme it had
    forced).

Great for scouting mazes and for checking whether a hunter is actually
in the corridor with you before you commit to a slide.

=======================================================================
12. TRAIL AND FLOOD-FILL  (maps and breadcrumbs)
=======================================================================

Three little helpers for not getting lost:

  B   Trail. Toggle a breadcrumb trail that follows your steps, so you
      can see where you came from.

  F   Flood-fill. Turns every walkable cell you can reach from your
      current spot blue - a live map of where you can and cannot go.

  Q   Clear. Removes the blue map and the trail.

These are especially handy in the mazes, to see whether a corridor is
a dead end before you wander in.


=======================================================================
13. THE TERMINAL
=======================================================================

Press T and a command line opens at the top of the screen. Type
commands with a slash. The most useful ones for everyday playing:

  /help                 Shows the list of all commands

  /seed 12345           Build a world from a specific seed (your own
                        personal world you can return to any time)

  /mode maze            Jump straight to a specific world type

  /speed 2              Walk faster or slower

  /enemies off          Turn hunters off
  /enemies 3            Set how many hunters chase you

  /light 12             Lantern radius - how big the light pool is

  /ice                  Ice mode - slides until you hit a wall

  /vision              Vision mode - the ray-cast radar
  /vision 128          Set the radar range in cells (8-256)

  /ss                   Take a screenshot (saves a PNG image)

  /share                Copy a link to this exact world to the
                        clipboard (seed, mode, position and settings).
                        Add -s for a short peer link (~12 chars) that
                        streams the state live from this tab over the
                        network (WebRTC/PeerJS) - keep this tab open or
                        the link dies. Bare /share works fully offline.

  /locate portals       Find the nearest portal (how far away it is)

  /gamerule spawn_portals 0.05
                        Portal chance per chunk - how often you meet one
                        (0 = none, 1 = everywhere). Set this any time.

  /lock seed mode       Lock settings so they cannot be changed by
                        keyboard or terminal. /lock all locks
                        everything, /unlock all unlocks everything.

  /gm hunt              Apply a preset (try /gm ? to see them all).
                        Presets lock all settings at once.

  /vol 0.5              Set the volume (0 = mute, 1 = full)

  /rule                 Shows the tuning dials (enemy count, miss
                        chance, goal distance, ...). Read-only unless
                        you know what you are doing.

  /export 0 0 200 200 2 Saves a picture of a chunk of the world as a
                        PNG image file.

  /goto 1000 500        Jump the camera to a specific spot

  /d ?                  List the debug tools (hud, charts, watchdog,
                        stall, dbgdmp, perf, frames, term, clear,
                        defaults, fatal). /d defaults wipes saved
                        settings and resets to defaults.

  /info                 Show licence and build info

Press Escape to close the terminal. While typing, Tab completes the
command for you, and the up/down arrows recall what you typed before.

NUMBERS YOU CAN TYPE

Wherever a command wants a number - a seed, a coordinate, a radius -
the terminal reads a few friendly spellings of it:

  64            plain digits, as always
  2k            2000     (k = thousand; M = million, G = billion,
                          T = trillion and so on up the SI ladder)
  5ki           5120     (ki = 1024; Mi, Gi, Ti... double the previous,
                          the computer's own ladder)
  0x1F          31       (0x = hexadecimal; 0b11 = 3 and 0o17 = 15 too)
  7.5e9         7500000000   (e9 = "times a 1 with nine zeros")
  2w65536       2 to the power of 65536 (w = power of two)
  4t2           400      (t2 = "times 10 squared")
  mmax          the largest whole number this browser can hold

Two rules worth knowing. A marker letter only starts an exponent when a
digit follows it right away: 5Y3 is 5 times 16 cubed, while a bare 5Y
is the SI prefix yotta. And a decimal point is allowed only when the
answer lands on a whole number of cells: 1.5e3 is 1500, but 1.5e0 is
turned away (that is 1.5, not a cell).

The world gets absurdly large, and huge coordinates are printed in a
short scientific shape like 6.338'25 with a power of ten attached. That
printed form is a legal number too - /goto ? tells you where you are in
exactly it, and typing it back into /goto lands you there again (give
or take a cell at those distances; the display keeps only the first few
digits).

/goto also understands relative moves, so you can nudge instead of
teleporting:

  /goto ~4 ~-60    4 cells east and 60 north of where you stand now
  /goto ~4~-60     the same move written as a single word
  /goto ~10        10 cells east and 10 north
  /goto O          back to the origin (0, 0)

WHAT ARE THE PORTALS?

Every so often the ground is torn open: a dark vent of swirling shards,
like a rip in the fabric of the world. That is a portal, and it is
one-way. Walk into it and it sends you somewhere far away - no portal
comes back from the other side. Blame the distance: most portals are
1/8 likely to just drop you near where you started, but the rest fling
you out across the world's outer regions, sometimes millions of light
years out (that is what the "void" out there looks like). If you want
more or fewer of them, use /gamerule spawn_portals (0 stops them
completely). To find the nearest one, /locate portals.


=======================================================================
14. A GOOD WAY TO START
=======================================================================

If you are brand new, here is a gentle first route through the game:

  1. Open the file and just scroll around a bit. Press C a couple of
     times to find a color theme you like.

  2. Press F1+1 (a classic maze). Press F to flood-fill, then walk or
     pan around to admire how perfect and maze-like it is. Press Q to
     clean up.

  3. Press F4+4 (glyphs - arrow tiles) or F4+1 (tenprint) for
     neat patterns that are a joy to look at.

  4. Press F7+1 (cavern) - a friendly open cave. Press X for a goal
     and actually walk to it. Watch the star counter climb.

  5. When you feel ready, press E to bring in one hunter and keep
     walking between goals. Corners are your friends.

  6. Save your favourite world with /seed, and come back to it
     whenever you like.

           ground around you simply becomes the new world.

  SEED   - the secret number the mode cooks with. New seed = a fresh
           world of the same kind. Press R to re-roll.

So there are really 53 modes x endless seeds = endless worlds. Every
mode below starts from the same honest rule: either a cell is open
ground you can walk on, or it is a wall.

One handy thing to know before we start: in every mode except one, you
must walk along open ground - walls stop you. The single exception is
"random" (plain digit 1), where nothing stops you at all. That is
deliberate, and it will make sense when we get there.


=======================================================================
HOW TO SWITCH WORLDS
=======================================================================

The shortcuts are laid out so that related worlds share a "bin": hold
an F-key (or Shift / Ctrl / Shift+Ctrl) and press a digit 1-9 and you
jump straight there. Digit 0 and digits past a family's size do
nothing; F12+1 is the exp stress test (browsers may grab F12 first).

  F1 + 1 to 3           Perfect mazes (maze, braid, recursive)
  F2 + 1 to 4           Regular mazes (hilbert, hilbertinv, megamaze,
                        megamazep60)

  F3 + 1 to 3           Mine networks (mine, blobs, mineshaft)
  F4 + 1 to 4           Tiles and rooms (tenprint, truchet, qr, glyphs)

  F5 + 1                Snow field (snowyplanes)
  F6 + 1 to 2           Percolation at pc (critical, critbond)

  F7 + 1 to 3           Blob caves (cavern, plasma, dripstonecave)
  F8 + 1 to 7           Thin caves (cheese, spaghetti, noodle, pillar,
                        warp, carver, ravine)

F9 + 1                Mountain terrain (mountains)
F10 + 1 to 8          Cell-based (voronoi, cellular, cellnoise, conway, crypt, conwaygallery, babel, conwaygalleryhistory)
F11 + 1 to 6           Fractal & prime spirals (weierstrass, primespiral,
                         sachs, primespiraldense, gcd, chip)
F12 + 1                Stress-test (exp)

  SHIFT + 1 to 3        Rooms (chambers, uchambers, colapsedchambers)
  SHIFT+CTRL + 1         Composite worlds (underground)
  CTRL + 1 to 2         Forests (forest, forestcave)
  CTRL + 3 to 5         Settlements (metropolis, village, subdivisions)
  1                     Free world (random)

Forgot which digit does what? Press Shift+H for the full help screen,
or cycle through every world one at a time with O (Shift+O goes
backwards, Ctrl+O jumps to a random one).

Now, the grand tour. Each world gets a short story - what it is, what
it looks like, and why you might enjoy it.


=======================================================================
1. MAZES AND MINE NETWORKS      (F1 / F2 / F3 + digits)
=======================================================================

These are the puzzle worlds: twisty passages, deliberate dead ends,
and (in the mines) wide open galleries. Corridors are what you live on.

  F1+1  MAZE
     The classic. A perfect maze: every single path leads somewhere,
     and there is always a way out - you just have to find it. The
     corridors are one cell wide and studded with an endless supply of
     tiny dead ends. The place every explorer learns to flood-fill (F)
     and mark a trail (B). The gold standard.

  F1+2  BRAID
     Maze's friendlier cousin. Most of the dead ends have been opened
     up into loops, so it still looks like a maze but you rarely get
     cornered. The corridors are still one cell wide, but now they
     double back and meet themselves. Fewer traps, more flowing.

  F1+3  RECURSIVE
     A maze with mazes inside mazes. Corridors run at four different
     scales at once - think big main roads, side streets, alleyways,
     and cracks in the wall. Dead ends appear at every size. The
     classic "subdivided forever" look, and a joy to zoom into and out
     of.

  F2+3  MEGAMAZE
     A maze walled off into big chunks with thick borders. The borders
     are mostly full of doorways - but a door only lets you through if
     there is a matching one on the other side, so crossing between
     chunks is a rare treat. Feels like exploring a maze that is built
     one room at a time.

  F2+4  MEGAMAZE P60
     The same idea, but with much bigger chunks and roughly half the
     doorways (the "p60" is the 60% in the math). Bigger stretches of
     maze, rarer connections, more miles between exits. For when
     megamaze starts to feel small.

  F2+1  HILBERT
     This one is a single giant squiggle. The walls are drawn as a
     space-filling curve - one unbroken line that folds and folds and
     folds itself to fill the whole map. You walk in the thin gaps
     between the coils. Neat, hypnotic, and utterly constant: it has
     no seed, so R does nothing. The same pattern, forever.

  F2+2  HILBERT INV
     The opposite squiggle: now YOU walk the curve itself. A single
     1-wide path that winds through the whole world without ever
     crossing itself. There is exactly one way forward and one way
     back, and nothing else exists. The most confining walk in the
     game - and quietly the most soothing one.

  F3+1  MINE
     A proper mine complex. Round room-blobs on a rough grid, joined
     by thick, wobbling tunnels - a few long galleries, plenty of
     branches, a bit of timber here and there. The most "hand-dug"
     feeling world, and a great one for chasing goals through.

  F3+2  BLOBS
     A string of pearls. Round, smooth hollows linked by narrow
     1-2-cell necks, like soap bubbles touching. The tunnels never
     swell into fat intersections, so every room feels carved and
     separate. Quick to read, satisfying to thread.

  F3+3  MINESHAFT
     A whole mining network that keeps changing style on you. Huge
     districts, each one different: some are dense warrens of tiny
     rooms, some are big open halls with wide corridors, some are a
     fine grid of micro-chambers, and some are the standard lattice.
     Corridors sometimes run through a support post, always leaving a
     way around. The most varied single mode - you genuinely do not
     know what is over the next border.


=======================================================================
2. TILES AND ROOMS      (F4 / Shift + digits)
=======================================================================

Orderly, patterned, man-made worlds. Carpets, signs, rooms, and QR
codes. They are beautiful to look at in explorer mode and interesting
to walk through on foot.

  F4+1  TENPRINT
     The famous 10 PRINT pattern: a carpet of diagonal slashes, each
     8x8 block drawn as "/" or "\" at random. Dense - nearly nine
     tenths of it is wall - so walking it feels like threading a
     lattice. In lantern mode the endless diagonals read deeply odd.

  F4+2  TRUCHET
     Tenprint's airy cousin. Each 8x8 tile is one of several pretty
     patterns - arcs, diagonals, quarter-circles, checker quads - in
     random rotations, and all of it is open floor. Where tenprint
     feels like a fence, truchet feels like a dance floor. Great for
     simply flying the camera over.

  F4+3  QR
     Yes, those QR codes. The whole world is paved with giant QR codes,
     edge to edge with white gutters between them. Dark squares are
     walls, light squares are ground, so each code is a mini-maze you
     could (in theory) scan. The bullseye corners are unmistakable.
     A world that makes your phone nervous.

  F4+4  GLYPHS
     Arrow signs on a one-wide grid. Every cell edge points toward one
     of its four neighbours, so the paths carry a strong sense of
     direction - follow the arrows and the ground seems to want to
     lead you somewhere. Like a network of one-way signs, drawn as a
     world.

  SHIFT+1  CHAMBERS
     Rooms. Big square rooms - 32x32 - with thick walls between them,
     each wall holding a door slot that opens about four times out of
     ten. But here is the twist: a wall only lets you through where
     two neighbouring rooms both happen to have a door in it. So the
     rooms are real rooms, and some walls are simply sealed.

  SHIFT+2  MICRO-CHAMBERS
      The same room-and-door idea, shrunk down hard. Tiny 7x7 rooms,
      tiny single-cell doorways, walls everywhere else. Most of the
      world is solid rock with little hollows punched in it, joined by
      single-cell cracks. Intimate, dense, and surprisingly atmospheric
      - like a maze made of matchboxes.

   SHIFT+3  COLAPSEDCHAMBERS
      Micro-chambers with collapse zones. Most of the world is the
      same 7x7 room grid as uchambers, with tiny single-cell doorways
      connecting tiny rooms - but a slow fractal noise field
      occasionally peaks high enough to replace whole sections with
      solid rock. The solid blocks interrupt the regular room grid,
      forcing you to detour around them or find a rare crack through.
      Most of the time it reads like uchambers: intimate, dense,
      doorways everywhere. Then suddenly the rooms stop and a wall of
      stone fills the view. Like a matchbox maze with occasional
      collapsed ceilings.


=======================================================================
3. PEAKS, FRACTALS & PERCOLATION      (F5 / F6 / F9 / F11 + digits)
=======================================================================

A little row of strange worlds that do not fit the caves or the tiles.
Math made walkable.

  F9+1  MOUNTAINS
     Two worlds in one, split by a sharp treeline. Above the line, the
     peaks form a sparse, broken labyrinth of ridges - about one cell
     in five is open. Below it, a proper forest of trees and winding
     paths. Walk up, and the land turns to puzzle; walk down, and it
     turns to woods.

  F11+1  WEIERSTRASS
     A genuine mathematical monster: the Weierstrass function, the
     famous curve that is too jagged to ever have a smooth point.
     Drawn as a world, it makes bands and ridges at many scales at
     once - big bands of 128 cells, then 42, then 14, then smaller
     still, all piled on top of each other. Rough, spiky, endlessly
      interesting to zoom through.

  F11+2  PRIMESPIRAL
     The Ulam spiral made physical. Numbers spiral outward from the
     center, and wherever a number is prime, a wall appears. The result
     is thin walls that follow the famous diagonal lines where primes
     cluster, with wide passages between them. The spiral structure
     gives you just enough orientation to stay oriented.

  F11+3  SACHS
     A radial cousin of primespiral. Numbers spiral outward on
     concentric rings, and walls appear where a number is prime or
     where both its neighbors are prime. The result is ring corridors
     at prime-numbered radii, connected by radial arms - a geometric
     pattern of circles and spokes, more open and regular than
     primespiral.

F11+4  PRIMESPIRALDENSE
     Like primespiral, but denser. Walls appear where a number is
     prime or where either neighbor is prime, creating thick connected
     corridors along the diagonal polynomial lines. About 30% of cells
     near the center are walls, so the spiral pattern is unmistakable
     even at a glance.

   F11+5  GCD
     The number-theory lattice. A cell is open exactly when its two
     coordinates are coprime - the greatest common divisor is 1. The
     result is a fine, even lattice of open ground about 61% dense,
     where walls appear only as small blotches where both coordinates
     share a factor. The lines x=-1, x=1, y=-1 and y=1 are fully open,
     so long sightlines run straight across the world and nothing can
     ever trap you. The pattern does not depend on the seed at all.

   F11+6  CHIP
     Silicon-wafer interference fringes. A cell is open (lit wafer band)
     exactly when the mod-2 parity of
     floor(floor(y) * (floor(x)*PI + e)) is even. Near the origin the
     slanted diagonal bands are wide and walkable, but the irrational
     product grows without bound, so the even/odd seams shear into
     fine 1-cell meanders as you travel outward - a faint endless
     lattice in the dark. Like gcd, it never depends on the seed.

   F6+2  CRITBOND
     A world balanced on a knife's edge. Every cell joins its
     neighbours through open or closed bonds at exactly the moment the
     network is about to fall apart - the percolation tipping point.
     The result: fragile patches of open ground that only just barely
     hold together, like cracks in a frozen pond. A scattered,
     half-formed maze that changes character with every seed.

  F5+1  SNOWYPLANES
     A snowy plain, freshly fallen. Almost the whole world is open,
     flat, white ground - but here and there, where the snowfall ran
     deepest, a rare drift rises out of the snow: a single mound of
     wall you have to walk around. It is the emptiest world in the
     game, a place for calm, long strolls across an endless field of
     white.


=======================================================================
4. CAVES      (F7 / F8 + digits)
=======================================================================

Soft, organic, natural-looking caves. These are the classic "worm a
tunnel through stone" worlds, and the best place to start if you just
want a pretty walk.

  F7+1  CAVERN
     The friendly cave. Big open blobs with smooth, curving walls,
     carved by simple gentle noise. Nothing sharp, nothing cramped -
     just honest cave, the kind a child draws. The recommended first
     world. Press X, put down a goal, and take a long walk.

  F7+2  PLASMA
     Cavern's softer sibling: the same cave blobs, but rounder and
     calmer, with the hollows nudged toward the middle of each area.
     Fewer sharp nooks, more comfortable open space. A good warm-up
     world.

  F8+1  CHEESE
     Swiss cheese as a cave. Lots of small, round pocket-rooms, joined
     by narrow mouths, with plenty of solid rock between them. It is a
     labyrinth of little chambers - like a honeycomb eaten halfway.
     Every room is a hiding place, which makes it a sneaky one in
     lantern mode.

  F8+2  SPAGHETTI
     A plate of it. Two separate fields of twisty noodles wound into
     each other, so the open ground is a tangled net of 1-2-wide
     corridors threading through thick wall. Turns constantly, crosses
     itself, never goes straight for long. Fast and confusing to walk
     - exactly the point.

  F8+3  NOODLE
     Just one noodle. A single thin band of open ground, one or two
     cells wide, snaking through endless solid rock. There is no
     branching, no choice, no escape - just follow the noodle. It is
     beautiful, and it is officially the scariest world in the game
     when the lantern goes out. Walk it if you dare.

  F8+4  PILLAR
     Cheese cave, but somebody dropped in the columns. Wherever the
     cave would have left a pocket of open space, a second, slower
     pattern sometimes grows a thick round pillar right in the middle
     of it. Result: rooms with hidden blind corners everywhere you
     turn. A cave that plays tricks on you.

  F8+6  CARVER
     A wandering line carved into stone. The open path is a thin
     contour that follows the terrain, constantly changing width,
     doubling back on itself, and meandering like a river that forgot
     where the sea is. Following it feels like tracing a coastline.

  F8+7  RAVINE
     Canyons. The ground is carved into long, parallel gorges by
     noise that is stretched sideways until it is all ridge. Standing
     in one you can see a very, very long way along it, and only a
     wall of rock at each end. Great sightlines, a strong sense of
     direction, and a striking world to fly over.

  F8+5  WARP
     Caves that fold. Before the stone is carved, the whole map is
     twisted and curled by a slow invisible field, so the caves bend
     and wrap back on themselves like taffy. Every passage is
     constantly turning, and you can never see far ahead. The
     opposite of ravine: no sightlines at all, just surprise after
     surprise.

  F7+3  DRIPSTONE CAVE
     Cave with decorations. The walls are studded with clusters of
     dripstone - stalagmites and stalactites - poking through the
     surface wherever the stone grew deep. Imagine a normal cave, then
     imagine it dripping. One of the prettiest caves to wander slowly
     through.


=======================================================================
5. CELLS, FORESTS, SETTLEMENTS & COMPOSITE WORLDS      (F6 / F10 / Ctrl / Shift+Ctrl + digits)
=======================================================================

The big, dramatic worlds. Built from several patterns at once, they
reward a slow look and a long walk.

  F10+1  VORONOI
     Geometry as a cave. The world is a diagram of polygonal cells -
     like a honeycomb made of irregular stones - where every polygon
     has a rock pillar at its centre ringed by open floor, and the
     walls run along the straight edges between cells. Hard corners,
     blind pillars, and a map that looks engineered. Gorgeous from
     above, tricky on foot.

  F10+2  CELLULAR
     The cave with smoothed edges. The map is drawn coarsely and then
     sanded: every nook and jagged tooth is filed away until only
     wide, rounded caves with soft walls remain. The most comfortable,
     most open-feeling cave in the family - a lovely place to put down
     a goal and just cruise.

  F10+3  CELLNOISE
     Caves drawn with a fat crayon. Huge, coarse regions of open floor
     alternate with equally huge masses of wall, in broad blobby
     steps - like a map drawn on a very low-resolution screen and then
     enlarged. Simple, chunky, and strangely satisfying to stroll
     through.

  F10+4  CONWAY
     A living world turned to stone. Start with pure random noise, let
     Conway's Game of Life churn it for two hundred generations, then
     flip it over: everything that died becomes open floor, everything
     that survived becomes a wall. By gen 200 the soup has mostly
     collapsed, so the world reads ~93% open - a wide plain scattered
     with the sparse ash of the dead field: little still-lifes,
     blinkers, and soft clumps of debris that block your sightlines on
     every side. Almost empty, yet strangely cellular; every direction
looks like the one you just left. (Fresh areas cost a ~2s
      one-time draw as you walk in.)

  F10+5  CRYPT
     A library of every possible world-block, in cipher order. The
     map is a spiral of 16x16 square tiles, and the tile at step n is
     the world-seed encryption of n - a cipher that is a clean
     one-to-one shuffle, so walk the spiral outward and you pass each
     of the 2^256 possible 16x16 grids exactly once, in the order
     your seed picks. Open three-cell aisles ring every block, so from
     above it reads like a library floor of tiny rooms you can walk
     between. Block zero at the origin is an open plaza where you land;
     beyond it every tile is roughly half open. (Each block is drawn
     on first sight - a quick one-time draw per fresh area.)

  F10+6  CONWAYGALLERY
     The cipher library, but left running. Each 16x16 block keeps the
     cipher-order layout of CRYPT, but its pattern is a Game-of-Life
     world evolved on a loop - every block is a torus that wraps onto
     itself, so each one lives out its own life in isolation for a
     fixed number of generations before the survivors are turned to
     stone. The soup of a cipher block collapses into flickering
     still-lifes, blinkers, gliders and ash, all ringed by the same
     three-cell aisles you can walk. Those aisles are dotted: a line
     of wall-dots runs down the middle of every gutter. The origin
     stays an open plaza. Set the generation count with /rule
     conway_gol_iters (0 prints the raw cipher block again).

  F10+7  BABEL
     Every row of the world is one book, and every step you take to
     the right reads on through it. Letters are stamped into the
     floor in a small 8x8 pixel font, one world-cell per pixel, so a
     line of text is 8 cells tall and you can stride along entire
     books. Each book is the seed encryption of its own row number -
     all ninety-five printable characters, in cipher order - so the
     library is infinite and every row you cross spells a different
     passage. Two aisles run between the lines of text: an eight-cell
     band under the letters, and a one-cell gap between every
     character, so you can always walk the page. Nothing in it is
     meant to be read; it is a text you wander through.

  F10+8  CONWAYGALLERYHISTORY
     The cipher library left running, with time laid out flat. Each
     block still is a Game-of-Life world on a loop, but instead of
     showing only where it ends up, the map unrolls its whole life to
     the right: one 16x16 frame per generation, so a single world
     spreads across a strip of tiles - generation 0 (the raw cipher,
     turned inside out), then 1, then 2, all the way to the last one.
     Walk right and you watch a world evolve generation by generation,
     then step into the next world's strip and watch that one. Stand
     still and look up or down and you are seeing the same moment in
     time of the worlds stacked above and below. Same dotted aisles
     ring every frame. Set the length with /rule conway_gol_iters; the
     strip is that many generations plus the starting frame. The origin
     is an open plaza.

  CTRL+1  FOREST
     A real forest. Tall trees grow in clumps on a rough lattice, with
     about seven in ten open, and winding paths carved between them.
     It feels alive: dappled, irregular, full of little clearings.
     If you have ever wanted to walk through a procedural wood, this
     is the mode.

  CTRL+2  FORESTCAVE
     A cave that grew a forest. The normal cave stone is there, but
     patches of trees carve galleries right into the walls, opening
     the rock up into great mossy chambers. Stone and wood mixed, with
     all the quiet atmosphere of both. A wonderful middle ground
     between the caves and the forest.

  CTRL+3  METROPOLIS
     A city, but never on a grid. The ground is divided into irregular
     blocks by a diagram of winding cell-edges, and those edges become
     the streets. Each block is a solid building you walk around, with
     a thin sidewalk ring, most blocks carrying a small open courtyard
     in the middle, and roughly one block in ten thrown open as a
     public square. Nothing repeats: the streets curve, the blocks
     differ in size and angle, and every seed lays out a different
     town plan.

  CTRL+4  VILLAGE
     Open countryside. Big, wide fields lie between thin hedgerows
     that follow the same kind of winding cell-edges as metropolis,
     but here the hedges are broken - only about half of each hedge
     is solid, so you slip through the gaps like an open gate. Small
     cottages sit near the centre of about half the fields. Airy and
     hand-drawn, the calmest of the three settlements.

  CTRL+5  SUBDIVISIONS
     An administrative warren. The whole map is divided again and
     again into quarters - but each division is chosen by the seed,
     so regions come in every size from huge halls down to little
     offices, and the walls almost never line up across the whole map.
     Open rooms behind one-cell walls, with doorways punched near the
     middle of each edge. Endless bureaucracy, and somehow never the
     same twice.

  F6+1  CRITICAL
     A world right at the edge of collapse. Every cell is open or
     closed by pure chance at exactly the magic probability where
     clusters of open ground are just barely big enough to span the
     map - the critical point. The result is fractal chaos: giant
     jagged blobs of floor separated by hair-thin necks and tiny
     islands, everywhere. Raw, wild, and always on the verge of
     falling apart. A chaos you can walk.

  1  RANDOM
     The one wild card. Every single cell is a coin flip - open or
     wall, completely at random, with no structure anywhere. This is
     also the only free mode: walls do not stop you, so you can glide
     through the clutter like a ghost. It exists to show you what
     "no recipe at all" looks like. After a few seconds of this,
     every other world will feel like architecture.

  SHIFT+CTRL+1  UNDERGROUND
     A whole continent in one mode. The world is divided into organic,
     folded regions, each with its own terrain: great open halls, a
     forested cave, sealed micro-chambers, dripstone caverns, and
     networks of thin connecting tunnels. Wander far enough and the
     ground simply changes character around you. The richest world to
     explore on foot - every boundary is a new adventure.

  SHIFT+CTRL+2  BIOMES
     Four climates, one world. A slow continental field decides which
     of four styles you are standing in - wide cavern blobs, cheesy
     pocket rooms, dripstone rims, or forest glades - and the borders
     between them stay soft and porous so you can always wander
     through. The natural partner to underground: where that one
     switches in folds, this one blends in broad, gentle regions.

  F12+1  EXP
     The lab behind the lab. This world is not built to be walked - it
     is a stress test: each cell runs a deliberately slow piece of
     modular maths, so the terrain takes its sweet time appearing and
     the chunk workers sweat. A dense ~3/4-open lattice, uneventful to
     cross when it does render. Open it to feel how fast the engine can
     fill a screen; then press O to go somewhere friendlier. (Browsers
     often grab F12 for their own devtools, so /mode exp or the O key
     is the reliable way in.)

=======================================================================
WHERE TO GO NEXT
=======================================================================

Still not sure where to begin? Three gentle first stops:

  - Press F1+1 (maze), press F to flood-fill, and watch how the blue map
    shows you every reachable path. Press Q to tidy up.

  - Press F7+1 (cavern) for the friendliest cave, then Ctrl+1
    (forest) to see the same engine grow trees.

  - Press O and just flick through everything - the game will show you
    a random corner of each world. Stop when one makes you say "whoa".

If you want the how-it-works side of things, the full technical
descriptions of every mode live in docs/modes.md. But honestly, the
worlds explain themselves. Happy wandering.
